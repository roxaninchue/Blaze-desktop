# Research brief (competitors and pricing)

One page the seller can decide from. Built on the deep-research skill: load it (`load_skill deep-research`) and follow its sourcing rules (open pages, cite every number, date time-sensitive facts).

## 1. Frame
- Product (or product type), the seller's market and currency, sales channels (Shopify store, Instagram, WhatsApp, Facebook Marketplace, Jumia), and the decision: price it, stock it or skip it, or how to stand out.
- Their landed cost if known (supplier price + shipping + fees).

## 2. Find competitors (5-8)
- Search the same product on Google Shopping, Amazon, Jumia/Takealot/local shops, TikTok Shop, Instagram/Facebook sellers in the market, and other Shopify stores (`"product name" site:myshopify.com`, `inurl:products`).
- For each: name and URL, exact price and currency (with date), variants, shipping cost and time to the seller's market, offer (bundle, free shipping), review count and average, positioning in one line (cheap, premium, gift), and visible weaknesses (bad photos, slow shipping, complaints in reviews).
- Prefer the live product page over listicles; note when a price is a sale price.

## 3. Analyse
- Price range (min, median, max) converted to the seller's currency with the rate and date.
- Saturation signal: many identical listings with the same supplier photos = commoditised; compete on bundle, speed, local trust or content.
- Gaps: complaints to fix, missing variants, no local delivery, weak content.
- Recommended price with the maths: landed cost, target margin, where it sits in the range, and a launch offer if useful.

## 4. Write the one-pager (`research/<product>-brief.md`)
Sections: **Decision** (one line) · **Price range** table · **Competitors** table (name, price, shipping, reviews, angle, link) · **Gaps to win** (3 bullets) · **Recommended price and offer** · **Risks** (2-3) · **Sources** (every URL with access date). Keep it to one screen of reading.
